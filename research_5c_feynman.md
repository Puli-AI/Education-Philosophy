
# The User's 5C Model

The user's 5C model is defined as follows:

1.  **Curiosity**: Triggers the need to solve a problem or learn something.
2.  **Complex Problem Solving** (Core Element)
3.  **Creativity** (Core Element)
4.  **Critical Thinking** (Core Element)
5.  **Communication**: Communicating solutions or lessons learned, shared with others and/or executing solutions to test problem resolution.

This model appears to be a custom framework focused on a learning and problem-solving process, distinct from other established '5C models' found in general web searches (e.g., marketing, vaccine hesitancy, etc.).




# The Feynman Technique

The Feynman Technique is a four-step learning method developed by Nobel laureate Richard Feynman, aimed at achieving deep understanding of any subject. The steps are:

1.  **Select a concept and map your knowledge**: Start with a blank page and write down everything you know about the chosen topic. Use different colors for new information as you learn, creating a visual map of your understanding.
2.  **Teach it to a 12-year-old**: Explain the concept in simple terms, as if teaching it to a child. This step helps reveal gaps in understanding, as complexity and jargon often mask a lack of true comprehension. Writing it down helps organize thoughts and makes mistakes clear.
3.  **Review and Refine**: Review your explanation to identify areas where your understanding is weak. Return to the source material to study these sections until you can explain them simply. Rewrite sections as your understanding improves.
4.  **Test and Archive**: Test your understanding by teaching someone else without notes, referring to them only if necessary. Once satisfied, archive the simplified explanation for periodic review. This technique is a foundation for accelerated learning and lasting retention.
